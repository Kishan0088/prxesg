01 — The problem

ESG communication is broken
at three layers at once.

Most companies are either invisible — under-communicating real climate work — or exposed — over-claiming and getting caught. Both destroy stakeholder trust. The root cause is the same: there is no infrastructure for ESG communication.

Layer 01 / Internal

Your own claims contradict each other.

The annual report says one number. LinkedIn says another. The CEO interview says a third. Sales decks reference a fourth. No team is mapping these back to the data — until a regulator, buyer, or NGO does it for you.

Average enterprise has 200+ unreconciled public ESG claims
Layer 02 / Peers

Competitors are repositioning, quarterly.

Peer sustainability narratives shift continuously. New commitments, new framings, new claims, new silences. Most companies discover the shift six months late — usually through a buyer questionnaire or an investor's offhand comment.

Most CSOs review peers once a year. Peers change every quarter.
Layer 03 / External

The world is talking. You're not listening.

NGOs, regulators, social media, trade press, activist investors. ESG-material coverage about your company is being read by your investors and buyers — weeks before it reaches your sustainability team's inbox.

Stakeholders find ESG signals 3–8 weeks before internal teams do
02 — Why now

The cost of getting ESG communication wrong
is no longer measured in reputation.

It is measured in denied export contracts, repriced capital, regulator inquiries, and multi-year brand damage. The regulatory floor has risen everywhere — and the verification burden now sits on the company making the claim.

FY 2026 — 27India · SEBI

BRSR Core mandate expands to top 1,000 listed companies

Third-party assurance becomes mandatory across 49 KPIs covering emissions, water, waste, workforce, and governance. Every assured number becomes a permanent claim.

In force nowEU · Regulators

CBAM scrutiny on Indian exporters

Steel, cement, aluminum, fertilizer, hydrogen, and electricity exporters facing EU buyer requirements for verified emissions data and credible decarbonization narrative. Communication failure = lost contract.

2026 — 2027EU · UK · India

Green Claims Directive and equivalent rules tighten

Every environmental claim must be substantiated against an evidence base — or removed. ASCI in India, the FTC in the US, and the EU Commission are converging on the same standard.

Already activeISSB · 40+ jurisdictions

IFRS S1/S2 climate disclosure adoption

From UK, Australia, Japan, and Singapore through to GCC and Brazil — climate-related financial disclosures are now jurisdictional law, not voluntary practice. Investor questions are getting sharper.

Rising globallyCourts · Markets

Greenwashing litigation and greenhushing risk

Companies that overclaim get sued. Companies that say nothing lose investor coverage and procurement credibility. The middle path requires intelligence, not instinct.

03 — Our insight

Three signals exist today.
No one has integrated them.

Reporting software handles the data. Reputation intelligence handles the outside view. PR tools handle generic media. None of them speak to each other. None of them understand ESG communication as a coherent discipline. PRXESG sits in the seam — and the integration is the product.

05 — Integration

When the three signals contradict,
we surface it before stakeholders do.

The reason most companies fail at ESG communication is not lack of effort.

It is that the data lives in three silos: their own reporting team, their PR agency's media monitoring, and a research analyst's quarterly peer scan. By the time these three views meet — usually in a crisis — it is too late.

PRXESG closes the loop. When your stated claim contradicts your peer's stated claim, when external coverage flags a gap in your disclosure, when a regulator statement changes what you can safely say next quarter — the platform surfaces the connection in one workflow.

This is the product. The integration is the moat.
