The Problem

The data exists. The intelligence doesn't.

A decade ago, sustainability was a report. Today it's a live, multi-front risk surface — and a single contradiction on any one front can trigger losses across all of them.

The risk surface

ESG risk is now a board-level exposure.

Six fronts, one surface. No company has a single system watching all of it.

Regulatory

BRSR Core, CSRD, IFRS S1/S2, SEC climate, CBAM — mandatory, assured disclosure with real penalties for getting it wrong.

Greenwashing

Regulators and watchdogs now prosecute unsubstantiated claims; every public statement is a potential liability.

Reputational

An NGO report, a journalist, a viral post — damage compounds in hours while response cycles run in weeks.

Climate & physical

Transition and physical climate risk now flow into credit ratings, insurance, and investor scrutiny of resilience.

Supply chain

Forced labour, deforestation and emissions deep in the supply chain become the parent company's problem.

Litigation & investor

Climate litigation, shareholder resolutions, and rating downgrades turn ESG gaps into direct financial consequences.

Why it goes wrong

The signals are there. Nobody holds both sides.

Response takes weeks

An EU verifier query, a journalist, an NGO report — each triggers a scramble across consultants, lawyers and agencies. By the time a company responds, the damage is done.

Every claim is contested

Greenwashing enforcement on one side, greenhushing fear on the other. No company knows what it has already said — or whether its own filings contradict each other.

Signals are fragmented

Claims live across filings, decks, calls and websites; evidence lives in news, satellites and customs data. Nobody holds both sides in one place to reconcile them.

Nobody watches in real time

Rating agencies are backward-looking. PR firms have no data. The contradiction surfaces in the press before the company even knows it exists.

The cost of the gap

The contradiction wins the race.

Today, without one system, the story outruns the response — every time.
